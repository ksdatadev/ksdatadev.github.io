AI in Accounts Payable: what will it really cost?

A Monte Carlo simulator comparing the total cost of running accounts payable with a human-centric workflow and with an AI-agent-centric workflow. Rather than one static business case, the model draws invoice volumes, exception rates, payment-error rates, token prices and project overruns from eight probability distributions, then runs thousands of scenarios to show the range of outcomes. Change any assumption and run it again.

How the model works

  1. Invoice volume. Each month, volume = starting invoices × (1 + monthly growth)month × a draw from the monthly volume distribution.
  2. Human-centric staffing. Clerk headcount = volume ÷ staffing ratio, rounded. Invoices above clerk capacity (throughput × working days) are processed on overtime. Each increase in headcount incurs a hiring cost. Headcount reductions are free.
  3. Exceptions. A share of invoices needs supervisor investigation, drawn monthly. Investigations above supervisor capacity are worked as overtime.
  4. Payment errors. A share of invoices is paid wrongly, drawn monthly, each costing a fixed average loss.
  5. AI-agent-centric. Clerks are removed and supervisors handle exceptions. Costs added: implementation budgets (each scaled by an overrun draw), token cost per invoice (scaled by an annual token-price change draw), optional platform run cost and transition cost, and an optional low-probability outage that delays a share of a month's payments.
  6. Salaries inflate monthly at the annual inflation rate ÷ 12. Losses from payment errors are held at nominal value.
  7. Persistence switches. By default every distribution is drawn independently each month, as in the original model. In practice an agent that misbehaves in one month tends to misbehave in the next. Ticking a persistence switch draws that parameter once per scenario, which widens the range of outcomes considerably.

Methodology reverse-engineered from Modeling AI investment returns under uncertainty (FA CFO Summit, Chicago, November 2026). Defaults reproduce that model's structure. This is an illustrative planning tool, not financial advice. Calibrate every assumption to your own organisation.
